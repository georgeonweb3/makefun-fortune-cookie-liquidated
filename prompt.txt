Build notes — make.fun "Fortune Cookie for the Liquidated Trader", slug u-clever-harbor-75-fortune-cookie-for-the-liquidated-38fd239c

What to build: a Python 3.13 program, main.py, that writes exactly one
fortune cookie message to /output/entry.txt. The line must be UTF-8,
between 1 and 160 characters, at most 20 words of plain text, and the
run must be deterministic with no network.

How the judges score it, in their order:
1. It reads as a fortune cookie message for a crypto trader who was
   just liquidated, with the cookie voice and the trading loss both
   in it; a line with no loss and no trader in it loses to any
   fortune about a liquidated crypto trader.
2. It makes a liquidated crypto trader laugh rather than cry: the
   joke lands on the loss and on the trader, rueful and warm, never
   cruel and never smug.
3. It is one line, at most 20 words, and it reads whole in a single
   breath the way a real fortune does.
4. The joke is fresh: a turn, a double meaning or a surprising last
   word, not the obvious liquidation pun anyone would reach for.
5. It keeps the fortune voice: small, oracular, slightly formal
   phrasing that the punchline then breaks.
Pairs get judged in both orders, margin 1-5; an exact tie goes to
the shorter text, so brevity is worth points. House lines of all
strengths play too, and no generic line may be copied word for word.

What the winners of the nearest judged one-liner contests teach:
"Six Words, One Story" (1,468 entries) was topped by 33-47 byte
lines whose twist lands late and hard. The lesson carried over here
is one breath, one twist, no filler — and concrete anchors (a time,
a named thing, a number) beat abstract wordplay.

Six lines were drafted and scored in a 15-pair round-robin against
the five criteria above:
A. A tall stranger, also liquidated, splitting one ramen noodle in
   silence — warm, but the payoff is quiet and the fortune voice
   is thin.
B. Framing the liquidation email and reading it aloud every night,
   like scripture — a strong ritual image, but it dwells on the
   loss instead of laughing at it.
C. At 3:12 a.m. the stop-loss sleeps, the long dies, and the
   exchange sends flowers shaped like the balance: zero — the
   densest anchors of the six, three clear beats, and the punch
   lands on the last word.
D. A 100x long meeting its maintenance margin at 2 a.m., the
   funding rate sending its regards in writing — specific, but
   the ending is flat and reads like a memo, not a fortune.
E. The cookie crumbles, the chart bleeds red, and the Lamborghini
   fund now buys exactly one bus ticket home — a clean reversal,
   but the Lambo-to-bus turn is the familiar one everyone reaches
   for.
F. Great fortune in the couch cushions, because the exchange has
   already searched the wallet twice — a sharp joke, but thinner
   anchors than C and a weaker last word.

C won 5-0, E 4-1, F 3-2, B 2-3, A 1-4, D 0-5. C was picked
unchanged: 112 characters, 19 words. Its one weakness is fortune
voice — it reads more as a joke narrative than a classic "You
will..." fortune — but its specificity and payoff beat every rival
on concrete anchors, and the flowers-shaped-like-zero image is the
freshest turn of the six.

Ship: main.py at the repo root (validates the 1-160 character and
20-word gates, refuses to write a drifted line, reads the file back
before finishing), a README carrying the 3 live make.fun badges,
and an MIT LICENSE. Repo: georgeonweb3/makefun-fortune-cookie-liquidated.
